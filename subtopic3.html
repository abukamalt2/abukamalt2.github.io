Research by Tanya Abu Kamal · Exploring the intersection of skincare, health, and information technology

What is telemedicine?

URLhttps://www.aad.org/public/fad/digital-health/telemedicine
Site authorAmerican Academy of Dermatology Association
AbstractThis American Academy of Dermatology guide introduces telemedicine as a way for patients to communicate with dermatology professionals without always traveling to an office. It explains that remote visits may involve electronic communication and can be helpful for visible skin, hair, and nail concerns. The guide also makes an important distinction: some problems still require an in-person examination, especially when a suspicious spot needs closer assessment. This source is useful for explaining how cameras, internet connections, scheduling systems, and digital medical communication expand access to care. It highlights the convenience of remote consultations for people with transportation or geographic barriers while avoiding the claim that online care replaces all traditional appointments. In a skincare technology project, the article helps show how information technology affects not only consumer beauty routines but also real clinical services and patient decision-making.

AAD Teledermatology Standards

URLhttps://www.aad.org/member/practice/telederm/standards
Site authorAmerican Academy of Dermatology Association
AbstractThese professional standards describe how teledermatology platforms should support safe, high-quality patient care. The recommendations address access to board-certified dermatologists, patient choice, appropriate clinical relationships, and the option to receive in-person care when needed. They also identify technical expectations for virtual visits, including suitable video quality, medical documentation, and privacy-conscious communication. This is an especially strong source for a computer science project because it shows that a healthcare website is more than a video-chat window. Its software must help clinicians exchange information, maintain records, protect sensitive data, and make reliable decisions about when remote care is appropriate. The standards also warn against platforms that prioritize business goals over patient safety. Overall, the document provides a framework for evaluating digital dermatology services through technology design, security, professional accountability, and the needs of patients.
